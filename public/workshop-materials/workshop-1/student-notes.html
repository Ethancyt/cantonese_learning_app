BACKGROUND

1 Introduction to Cantonese
A language used in daily life
Cantonese is called 廣東話 (gwóng dūng wá). It belongs to the Yue group of Chinese varieties and is widely
used in Hong Kong, Macau, parts of southern China and overseas communities. Cantonese and Mandarin differ
in pronunciation, vocabulary and grammar. [1]

Who created Cantonese
No single person invented Cantonese. It developed over generations as communities moved, interacted and
changed the way they spoke. Scholars debate exactly when its earlier forms emerged. Historical China had many
speech varieties; people across China did not all speak modern Cantonese. [2]

How Chinese writing changed
A broad historical sequence is oracle-bone and bronze inscriptions, seal script, clerical script and regular script.
These styles overlapped, and older styles are still used in calligraphy. Running and cursive writing also
developed. [3]
Traditional and simplified characters are written forms. They are not names for spoken languages. Hong Kong
usually uses traditional characters. Mainland China standardised simplified forms through reforms in 1956 and
1964, building partly on earlier forms. [4]

Pronunciation and tones
Yale romanization uses Latin letters to show Cantonese pronunciation. Tone marks and a low-tone h help show
the pitch of each syllable. The letters are pronunciation cues, so some combinations sound different from
English. [6]

Meet the six tones
Changing the tone can change the meaning. The following words share the sound si but have different tones.

 Tone  ·  Yale and character  ·  Meaning  ·  Voice movement

1  ·  sī 詩  ·  poem  ·  high and level

2  ·  sí 史  ·  history  ·  rise to a high pitch

3  ·  si 試  ·  try / test  ·  middle and level

4  ·  sìh 時  ·  time  ·  low and falling

5  ·  síh 市  ·  market / city  ·  rise from a low pitch

6  ·  sih 事  ·  matter / affair  ·  low and level

Level means steady. Rising means your voice goes up. Falling means your voice goes down. The numbers label
this chart; the words throughout these notes use Yale tone marks.
The h after a vowel marks a low tone; it is not an extra breath. Compare hái and haih. An h at the beginning of a
syllable, as in hóu, is a consonant sound. [6]

Dialogue

Key phrases

 Chinese  ·  Yale pronunciation  ·  Meaning

 你好  ·  Néih hóu  ·  Hello

 大家好  ·  Daaih gā hóu  ·  Hello everyone

 早晨  ·  Jóu sàhn  ·  Good morning

 好高興識你  ·  Hóu gōu hing sīk néih  ·  Nice to meet you

 拜拜  ·  Bāai baai  ·  Bye-bye

 聽日見  ·  Tīng yaht gin  ·  See you tomorrow

Language notes
大家好 greets a group. Use 早晨 in the morning. 聽日見 fits when you expect to see the person tomorrow.
都 (dōu) means also or too here. You may hear the longer expression 好高興認識你 (hóu gōu hing yihng sīk
néih).

3 Asking and giving names
Dialogue
Daniel  ·  你好！你叫咩名呀？  ·  Néih hóu! Néih giu mē méng a?
Hello! What is your name?

Amina  ·  我叫 Amina。你呢？  ·  Ngóh giu Amina. Néih nē?
My name is Amina. And you?

Daniel  ·  我叫 Daniel。  ·  Ngóh giu Daniel.
My name is Daniel.

Key phrases

 Chinese  ·  Yale pronunciation  ·  Meaning

 你叫咩名呀？  ·  Néih giu mē méng a?  ·  What is your name?

 我叫＿＿。  ·  Ngóh giu ＿＿.  ·  My name is ＿＿.

 你呢？  ·  Néih nē?  ·  And you?

 你姓咩呀？  ·  Néih sing mē a?  ·  What is your surname?

 我姓＿＿。  ·  Ngóh sing ＿＿.  ·  My surname is ＿＿.

Language notes
我 + 叫 + name
Ngóh + giu + name
叫 means to be called. Say your own preferred name normally after the Cantonese frame. You do not need a
Chinese name.
姓 (sing) asks specifically about a family name or surname. You can also identify yourself with 我係 Amina
(Ngóh haih Amina), meaning I am Amina. For the question about your name, 我叫 Amina is a useful answer.

3. My surname is Chan. 我＿＿ Chan。 / Ngóh ＿＿ Chan.
4. Put the parts in order: Amina / giu / Ngóh

5. Write your answer to 你叫咩名呀？ Then ask a partner.

4 Where you are from
You can name a place you identify with. Your origin, citizenship, birthplace and current home may be different.
Amina and Daniel are fictional examples.

Dialogue
Daniel  ·  你係邊度人呀？  ·  Néih haih bīn douh yàhn a?
Where are you from?

Amina  ·  我係加納人。你呢？  ·  Ngóh haih Gā Naahp yàhn. Néih nē?
I am from Ghana. And you?

Daniel  ·  我係香港人。  ·  Ngóh haih Hēung Góng yàhn.
I am from Hong Kong.

Key phrases

 Chinese  ·  Yale pronunciation  ·  Meaning

 你係邊度人呀？  ·  Néih haih bīn douh yàhn a?  ·  Where are you from?

I am from ＿＿
 我係＿＿人。  ·  Ngóh haih ＿＿ yàhn.
I am a person from ＿＿.

 我係加納人。  ·  Ngóh haih Gā Naahp yàhn.  ·  I am Ghanaian / from Ghana.

 我係香港人。  ·  Ngóh haih Hēung Góng yàhn.  ·  I am from Hong Kong / a Hongkonger.

Other ways to say it

 Chinese  ·  Yale pronunciation  ·  Meaning

 你喺邊度嚟㗎？  ·  Néih hái bīn douh làih ga?  ·  Where do you come from?

 我喺加納嚟㗎。  ·  Ngóh hái Gā Naahp làih ga.  ·  I come from Ghana.

 我嚟自加納。  ·  Ngóh làih jih Gā Naahp.  ·  I am from Ghana. Another useful form.

Language notes
In the first pattern, 係 (haih) means am / are and 人 (yàhn) means person. In the second pattern, 喺 (hái)
introduces the place someone comes from. Keep the question and answer frames together.

5 Where you live
Dialogue
Amina  ·  你住喺邊度呀？  ·  Néih jyuh hái bīn douh a?
Where do you live?

Daniel  ·  我住喺旺角。你呢？  ·  Ngóh jyuh hái Wohng Gok. Néih nē?
I live in Mong Kok. And you?

Amina  ·  我住喺沙田。  ·  Ngóh jyuh hái Sā Tìhn.
I live in Sha Tin.

Key phrases

 Chinese  ·  Yale pronunciation  ·  Meaning

 你住喺邊度呀？  ·  Néih jyuh hái bīn douh a?  ·  Where do you live?

 我住喺＿＿。  ·  Ngóh jyuh hái ＿＿.  ·  I live in ＿＿.

 我住喺旺角。  ·  Ngóh jyuh hái Wohng Gok.  ·  I live in Mong Kok.

 我住喺沙田。  ·  Ngóh jyuh hái Sā Tìhn.  ·  I live in Sha Tin.

Language notes
我 + 住喺 + place
Ngóh + jyuh hái + place
住 (jyuh) means live. 喺 (hái) marks the location here. A city or area name is enough for this conversation. [7]

 Identity  ·  Location

 我係香港人。  ·  我住喺沙田。
 Ngóh haih Hēung Góng yàhn.  ·  Ngóh jyuh hái Sā Tìhn.
 I am from Hong Kong.  ·  I live in Sha Tin.

係 (haih) and 喺 (hái) sound different and do different jobs. Where you are from and where you live now are
separate questions.

Make it your own
我住喺＿＿＿＿＿＿＿＿。
